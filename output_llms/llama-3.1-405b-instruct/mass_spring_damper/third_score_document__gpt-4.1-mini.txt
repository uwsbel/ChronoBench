- **Use of `ChLinkTSDA.Initialize`:**  
  The method signature is:  
  ```python
  Initialize(body1, body2, bool, pos1, pos2)
  ```  
  where `pos1` and `pos2` are the attachment points in the local coordinates of each body.

- **Attachment Points:**  
  - For `spring_1`, initialized between `body_1` and `ground` with points `(0,0,0)` on `body_1` and `(-1,0,0)` on ground.  
    - `body_1` is at `(-1, -3, 0)`, ground at origin.  
    - The point `(0,0,0)` on `body_1` corresponds to its center, which is at `(-1,-3,0)` world coords.  
    - The point `(-1,0,0)` on ground is offset from ground's origin.  
    - This means the spring connects from `body_1` center to a point at `(-1,0,0)` on ground. This is a valid setup.

  - For `spring_2`, initialized between `body_1` and `body_2` with points `(0,0,0)` on `body_1` and `(3,0,0)` on `body_2`.  
    - `body_1` at `(-1,-3,0)`, `body_2` at `(2,-3,0)`.  
    - The point `(3,0,0)` on `body_2` is 3 units along x from its center, which is at `(2,-3,0)`. So world position is `(5,-3,0)`.  
    - The spring connects `body_1` center to a point 3 units along x on `body_2`. This is a bit unusual because the bodies are 3 units apart along x, so the spring is effectively longer than the distance between centers. This is not necessarily incorrect but may cause unexpected spring behavior.  
    - Usually, attachment points are chosen at the same relative positions or at least consistent with body geometry.

  - For `spring_3`, initialized between `body_2` and `body_3` with points `(0,0,0)` on `body_2` and `(3,0,0)` on `body_3`.  
    - `body_2` at `(2,-3,0)`, `body_3` at `(5,-3,0)`.

    - This again is unusual and may cause unrealistic spring behavior.
