### 2. Correctness (30 points)

- **Use of `ChLinkTSDA.Initialize`:** The method signature is `Initialize(body1, body2, bool, pos1, pos2)` where `pos1` and `pos2` are the attachment points in the local coordinate frames of the respective bodies.  
  - For `spring_1`, initialized between `body_1` and `ground` with points `(0,0,0)` and `(-1,0,0)`. This is acceptable if these points are local to each body. However, the ground is fixed at origin (default), but the visual sphere is added at `(-1,0,0)` in ground's frame, so the spring anchor at `(-1,0,0)` on ground is consistent.  
  - For `spring_2`, initialized between `body_1` and `body_2` with points `(0,0,0)` and `(1,0,0)`. Given `body_1` is at `(-1,-3,0)` and `body_2` at `(1,-3,0)`, the local points `(0,0,0)` and `(1,0,0)` may not correspond to the actual connection points. Usually, the points should be specified in the local frames of the bodies, so the anchor on `body_2` at `(1,0,0)` local seems off because the body is at `(1,-3,0)` world. This could cause the spring to be attached off the body geometry.  
  - For `spring_3`, initialized between `body_2` and `body_3` with points `(0,0,0)` and `(2,0,0)`. Similarly, `body_2` is at `(1,-3,0)` and `body_3` at `(3,-3,0)`. The local point `(2,0,0)` on `body_3` seems inconsistent because the body is at `(3,-3,0)` world, so the local point `(2,0,0)` is likely outside the body.  
  - This indicates a **logical error** in joint initialization: the local attachment points on bodies 2 and 3 are not correctly set relative to their local frames. They should likely be `(0,0,0)` or points corresponding to the connection points on the bodies.
