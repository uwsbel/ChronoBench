### 4. Efficiency (10 points)

- **Unnecessary Calculations or Redundancies:**  
  - The gravitational force calculation is done every step with nested loops over all body pairs, which is O(n²). For small numbers of bodies, this is acceptable, but for large particle counts, this is inefficient.  
  - The code calls `sys.GetBodies()` multiple times in the loop (once for kinetic energy, once for force accumulation). It would be more efficient to store the list once.  
  - The call to `body.EmptyAccumulators()` is done for all bodies, which is necessary before force accumulation.  
  - The emitter emits 2000 particles per second with a reservoir of 200 particles, which could cause the reservoir to empty quickly, but this is a simulation parameter choice, not efficiency.  
  - No caching or vectorized operations are used, but given PyChrono's API, this is typical.  

- **Optimization Opportunities:**  
  - Store `sys.GetBodies()` once per loop iteration to avoid repeated calls.  
  - Implement a minimum distance check to avoid force calculation for very close bodies or self-interaction (though self-interaction is avoided by combinations).  
  - Use spatial partitioning or approximate methods for gravitational force if performance is a concern (not required here).  

**Deduction:**  
- Two instances of minor inefficiency (repeated `sys.GetBodies()` calls and no caching): -3
