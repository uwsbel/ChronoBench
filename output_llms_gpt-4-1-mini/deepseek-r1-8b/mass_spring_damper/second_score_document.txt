### 2. Correctness (30 points)

- **Custom Force Functor Implementation:**

  - The `MySpringForce` class inherits from `chrono.ForceFunctor` and overrides `ComputeForce`.
  
  - However, the constructor `__init__` is missing. The class uses `self.spring_coef` and `self.damping_coef` but these are never set, so this will raise an `AttributeError`.
  
  - The return statement is `return chrono.ChVector3d(spring_force, damping_force)`. Both `spring_force` and `damping_force` are already `ChVector3d` objects (spring_force is a vector, damping_force is a vector scaled by velocity). Passing two vectors to `ChVector3d()` constructor is invalid. The correct approach is to return the sum of the two vectors: `spring_force + damping_force`.
  
  - The spring force calculation uses `(chrono.ChVector3d(0, 0, 0) - local_pos)`. This is acceptable if the spring is anchored at the origin in the local frame, but the force direction depends on the relative position between the two bodies at the attachment points. This may be oversimplified or incorrect depending on the frame of reference, but without more context, it is acceptable.
  
- **Initialization of `spring_2`:**

  - The `spring_2` is initialized with `spring_2.Initialize(body_2, ground, True, chrono.ChVector3d(0, 0, 0), chrono.ChVector3d(-1, 0, 0))`.
  
  - The last two parameters are the attachment points in the local frames of `body_2` and `ground`. The ground attachment point is `(-1, 0, 0)`, which is the same as for `spring_1`. This is acceptable.
  
- **Use of `SetForceFunctor`:**

  - The `SetForceFunctor` method is called with `MySpringForce(spring_coef, damping_coef)`, but since the class lacks an `__init__` method, this will fail.
  
- **Visual Shape Creation:**
